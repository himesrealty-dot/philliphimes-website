Weighting

The brand-new build is not a like-for-like sale

5711 White Birch Run went under construction in March and under contract in May. Because those buyers got in that early, they selected design features throughout. It is a true never-lived-in custom home, and there is a premium attached to that.

We generally do not treat a brand-new, never-occupied custom home the same as one that has already been lived in — even when the lived-in home is still relatively new. My clients cannot choose a single design feature at 27711.

I kept that comp in the set at reduced weight rather than removing it. Throwing it out entirely would thin the data. But it should not carry the same influence as the other three, and pulling it drops the median to $1,800,000.

Condition

The stronger comps are better finished than the subject

Stacking these homes by quality of construction and finish, several features in the higher sales are clearly superior to 27711:

  • Outdoor living and pool areas are noticeably stronger in several of the sales.
  • The subject has cool deck — the lowest-cost decking option. Several comps have better decking, including Pebbletech finishes.
  • Water features at the subject are basic spray jets, the least expensive option. Several comps have waterfalls or more substantial features.
  • One of the stronger comps includes a climate-controlled wine room.
  • Secondary bedrooms in a higher comp have designer mirrors rather than standard plate glass.
  • Circle driveways, gated entrances, outdoor kitchens, larger-format tile (18×36), curved design elements and higher-end interior finishes all appear in the stronger sales.

The kitchen at 27711 is roughly on par with some of this set. But overall the subject sits a step below the top of the group on finishes and outdoor living — nearer the middle-to-lower end than the top.

The closest comparable is the one with stamped concrete decking, the three-car garage, and finishes very close to the subject’s. It has no crown molding, same as 27711. That is the most directly comparable sale in the set.
One of the stronger comps competed directly against this house. It was on the market in the same window, around June, and won that buyer over 27711. That is the market telling us it was the superior property.

Method

Why the median and not the average

The average imports the top sale’s premium. 5611 N Ossineke closed at $2,000,000 and carries a lake view; 27711 does not. Averaging lets that one sale pull the entire number upward and quietly prices a water premium into a house that has no water. The median will not do that. The sale still counts, and it still sets the top of the range — it just cannot drag the middle.

The median also handles the new build the way it should be handled. Rather than deleting 5711 White Birch Run and losing a data point, it keeps the sale in the set at limited influence:

What it contributes

That is the whole point. The new construction comp adds value, at reduced weight — it lifts our number by $27,958 rather than by the full strength of a never-lived-in custom sale. Keeping it in at full weight would overstate what my buyers are getting; throwing it out would thin the data. The median does both jobs at once.

Where that leaves us

Stack these homes by construction quality and finish, and 27711 sits closer to the middle of this group than to the top. That puts it at the median — $1,827,958 — and our offer of $1,820,000 is within $8,000 of it.
