The short version

I recommend we list at $799,000.

The evidence puts the value of your home at about $785,000, in a range of $760,000 to $810,000. A list price of $799,000 sits inside that range, lets the market tell us quickly whether the top of it is real, and keeps your home in front of every buyer searching up to $800,000.

That is just under the $801,009 you paid in August 2021. I know that is not the number you were hoping to see. The rest of this page shows why I believe it is the right one, and why a higher asking price is likely to cost you money, not make it.

Step 6 · Position the home

What your home wins on, and what buyers will weigh.

Where you win

  • Built 2021. The newest resale under $1M in Augusta Pines.
  • Finished for today's buyer. Wide-plank wood floors, quartz, soft-close cabinetry, a linear fireplace and the glass wine wall.
  • Two bedrooms down. A real answer for multigenerational families.
  • A lower tax rate. About 2.39% here, against 2.46% to 3.02% in Woodson's Reserve.

What buyers will weigh

  • No pool. 19 of the 27 homes that sold in Augusta Pines had one.
  • Back yard privacy. The neighboring homes sit close on three sides.
  • Choices at lower prices. Older homes with pools are for sale in the low $700,000s.

The price has to account for both columns. The marketing leads with the first one: a modern home, ready to move into, in a neighborhood where nearly everything else under $1M was built before 2019.

Step 9 · The plan

Launch at $799,000 and let the first 10 days tell us.

  • Lead with what is new. The headline and first photos say built 2021 and show the modern interior before anything else.
  • Every showing gets a yes. The first 10 days bring the most buyers a listing will ever see. Homes on standard lots that sold here since April went under contract in a median of 7 days.
  • We have a yardstick. The average Augusta Pines listing drew about six showings in three months. If your home draws that many in its first 10 days, the price and the presentation are doing their job.
  • We review together at 10 days. If showings have not produced an offer, the market has answered, and I will recommend moving to about $779,000 before the listing goes stale.
  • We prepare for the appraisal now. I will hand the appraiser the Carolina Cherry sale, the Oxbow Lake sale and the newer resales nearby, with the adjustments shown here.
  • We favor the strongest buyer, not only the highest offer. A buyer who can cover a gap between the appraisal and the price is worth more than one who cannot.

I price to attract, not to leave room. Testing the market at too high a price for too long is expensive. These three sellers found that out.

  • 26006 Carolina Cherry, in your section. First asked $999,950 and sold nine months later for $755,000. At a $4,000 payment that is $36,000 spent waiting, and the price still came down $244,950.
  • 5361 Cherry Spring, built 2021. Asking $920,000 in February and sold for $827,000 after 495 days on the market. At $4,000 a month that is about $65,000 in payments.
  • 7407 Sawgrass Terrace, Augusta Pines, built 2021. Asking $1,225,000. It has been on the market 441 days, through two expired listings, and is still for sale.

None of them was paid more for waiting. That is why we look at the results together at 10 days. I would rather your home sell in its first weeks, at the strongest price the market has shown it will pay.
