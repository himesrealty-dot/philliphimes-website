Why You Need a REALTOR® for New Construction

The builder’s on-site sales agent has one job: maximize the builder’s profit. They’re professionals at it. Everything you learn about that home, that contract and that community comes from someone paid by the other side of the table.

Most production builders now build spec — homes designed, started and finished before a buyer ever walks in. It keeps their costs predictable and their schedules short. It also means you aren’t choosing finishes or negotiating options. You’re choosing between finished houses, and the leverage sits in price, terms, and knowing which house.

Phil has over 20 years in real estate, with experience as a mortgage lender, REALTOR® and investor. On a new build the negotiation is financing-shaped, so that second half matters as much as the first — and working with him doesn’t change the price you pay for the home or get you a worse deal.

You’re not doing this alone, trusting the builder to tell you what you need to know.

01
The Contract
Builder contracts are written by the industry’s best lawyers, and they are written to protect the builder. Misreading one can cost you your earnest money — or the house you already fell in love with. Phil reviews it with you so you understand the risks before you sign.
02
The Incentives
Builder incentives are complicated, and they are deliberately hard to compare from one builder to the next. Phil helps you understand what each one is actually worth, so you get the house you want at the best price and terms available.
03
Community Selection
A builder’s agent can only ever recommend their own community. Phil guides you through the selection — amenities, nearby businesses, commute times, and the lifestyle needs that decide whether a place actually fits.
04
Builder Track Record
You didn’t watch this house go up, so the builder’s record is your best read on what’s behind the drywall. Phil has firsthand experience with the major builders here — construction quality, customer service, and how they actually handle warranty claims.
05
School Zones
New communities sometimes straddle district boundaries. Phil verifies the exact school assignment for the specific address you’re considering — not the one on the community brochure.
06
Same Price, Either Way
Working with Phil doesn't change the price you pay for the home or get you a worse deal with the builder. How his commission is handled is part of the negotiation — so there's no reason to go in without representation.

Do I need a REALTOR® to buy new construction?

Yes — and working with your own REALTOR® doesn't change the price you pay or get you a worse deal. Builder sales agents work for the builder, not you. Bringing Phil gives you independent representation to negotiate price and terms, review the contract, and protect your interests through closing. How the buyer's-agent commission is handled is part of the agreement, and Phil explains it before you sign anything.

What new construction communities are near League City TX?

Active builder communities include Legacy, Westland Ranch, and Coastal Point in League City (CCISD); Legacy and Friendswood Trails in Friendswood (FISD); Massey Oaks and Alexander in Pearland (Alvin ISD); Peacock Isle in Dickinson (Dickinson ISD); Toll Brothers at Midline in Webster/Clear Lake (CCISD); and Valencia, Pomona, Meridiana, Del Bello Lakes, and Avellino in Manvel (Alvin ISD). Prices range from the mid $250s to $1M+ depending on community and builder.

How much does new construction cost near League City TX?

New construction starts as low as the mid $250s at Avellino in Manvel (KB Home). Westland Ranch in League City starts from the $270s. Manvel and Pearland communities (Meridiana, Pomona, Massey Oaks) typically run from the low $300s to high $400s. League City CCISD communities (Legacy, Coastal Point) start in the high $300s to $400s. Friendswood FISD new construction starts from the $400s to $600s. The floor plan, the lot premium, and the upgrade package already in a given house are what move it above that base price.

How long does it take to build a new home?

Production builders complete homes in about 4–6 months. A home already under construction when you contract will be shorter than that, and a finished quick move-in can close in 30–60 days. Phil tracks the timeline and attends the inspection and both walk-throughs.

Can I negotiate on a new construction home?

Yes — builders rarely reduce base price but regularly offer incentives: included upgrade packages, closing cost contributions, preferred-lender rate buydowns, or appliances. Leverage is highest at quarter-end when builders need to hit sales targets. Phil knows which builders are negotiating and when, and he has existing relationships that typically produce better outcomes than going in alone.
