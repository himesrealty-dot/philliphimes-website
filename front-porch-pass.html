Questions people ask

Is this the same as what my house is worth?

No, and the difference matters. The appraised value is what the county assessed your property at for tax purposes, as of 1 January. It is not a market valuation, it does not account for what you have done to the house, and it is usually conservative — in a rising market it tends to lag what a home would actually sell for. It is a good way to watch direction over time. It is not a number to price a sale on.

What is the difference between appraised and assessed?

Appraised is the county’s opinion of value. Assessed is the portion you are actually taxed on. If you have a homestead exemption, Texas caps how fast the assessed value can rise — no more than 10% a year — so in a fast market the two numbers pull apart. That gap is money you are not being taxed on.

I don’t have a homestead exemption. Can I still file?

If it is your principal residence, almost certainly yes — and Texas lets you file late. Under Tax Code §11.431 an appraisal district must accept a homestead application filed up to two years after the delinquency date, and if it is approved you get a refund of the taxes you already overpaid, due within 60 days. Plenty of people who bought recently never filed and have no idea.

Applications go to your appraisal district, not to me, and they are free. Anyone charging you to file one is charging you for a form.

My value went up a lot. Can I do anything?

You can protest it. Appraisal notices generally mail in April and the deadline is 15 May or 30 days after your notice, whichever is later. Comparable sales do most of the work in a residential protest — and pulling comps is my actual job, so if you want them, ask and I will run them.

What does this cost, and what happens to my details?

Nothing, and you do not have to give me anything to see your numbers — the lookup is free and the data is public record. If you decide you want the monthly note, I keep your name and email to send it, I do not sell or share them, and you can stop it any time by replying.
